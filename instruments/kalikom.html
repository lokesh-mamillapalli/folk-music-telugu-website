కాలికోం

Kalikom

Also known as Kalikom (alternative Telugu spelling) (కాళికోం), Kolikammu (కొలికమ్ము)

A long, curved horn of the Gonds of Adilabad, played by Pardhan musicians. It sounds together with the tudum drum and the pepre shawm in the Dandari–Gussadi festival and at the Nagoba Jatara, and its call is likened to a conch summoning people to battle.

About

The kalikom is a long, curved horn used by the Gonds of the Adilabad region of northern Telangana. It is named among the instruments of the Dandari–Gussadi festival and of the Nagoba Jatara, the annual pilgrimage of the Mesram clan of Gonds1,3,4,5.

A 2013 list of eighteen Gond instruments from Adilabad district records the kalikom, together with the sanai (shawm) and the kikiri fiddle, as an instrument of the Pardhans, the hereditary bards and musicians of the Gonds6. The kalikom was among the tribal and folk instruments shown at 'Adi Dhwani', a 2019 exhibition in Hyderabad of tribal and folk instruments, many of them collected by Professor Jayadheer Tirumala Rao7.

How it is made

The English Wikipedia describes the kalikom as a long, curved horn made of brass or wood1. No detailed published account of its size, making or makers was found during this research1.

How it is played

The kalikom is blown as a companion to drums. A Telugu description of the tudum drum says that when the tudum is played the kalikom is blown as an accompanying instrument2.

Haimendorf's account of the Dandari season in the 1940s describes a visiting troupe arriving at night led by torch-bearers and musicians, among them 'a Pardhan trumpeting on a huge metal horn', and leaving again 'to the beating of dapna and kettle drums and the shrieking blasts from the Pardhan's large horn'. He did not give the horn a name8.

Beliefs & practices

The combined sound of the tudum and the kalikom is said to feel like hearing a conch that calls people to get ready for battle; the Adivasis describe it as the tudum pealing out and the kalikom crying out2.

A 2013 list of Gond instruments names the kalikom among the instruments played by the Pardhans, and Haimendorf described the Pardhans as the hereditary bards who act as musicians at Gond rites, blowing their wind instruments at important moments6,8.

Where you will hear it

The kalikom is one of the instruments of the Dandari–Gussadi festival held around Diwali by the Raj Gonds and Kolams of Adilabad and neighbouring districts. Telugu and English accounts list it with the dappu, tudumu and pepre, and Village Square names it among the instruments used in Gussadi celebrations1,3,5.

At the Nagoba Jatara at Keslapur in Indervelli mandal, the Deccan Chronicle reported in 2025 that the kalikom, dhol, thudum and pepri are played differently to suit each ritual or occasion, their sound resounding across Keslapur4.

Research notes: points where sources differ or could not be confirmed
  • The kalikom is a horn of the kommu/kahala family covered on a separate page; it may be better presented there as the Gond form of the horn, or cross-linked.
  • Spelling varies: kalikom in English sources; కాలికోం (Telugu Wikipedia, Tudum) and కాళికోం (Telugu Wikipedia, Gussadi list). The Telugu Wikipedia Gussadi text also names a 'కొలికమ్ము' (kolikammu), which appears to be the same instrument, but this was not confirmed.
  • Mikkilineni Radhakrishna Murthy (1992) lists a 'కలికొయ్' (kalikoy) among Gusadi instruments but glosses it as a తప్పెట (frame drum); it is unclear whether this is a different instrument or an error.
  • Haimendorf (1948) describes a Pardhan's 'huge metal horn' in Dandari processions and illustrates Pardhan wind instruments called pepre and bonga, but never uses the name kalikom; whether his horn or the bonga is the kalikom was not confirmed.
  • The material ('brass or wood') comes only from the uncited English Wikipedia table; no reliable written description of its construction was found.
  • Weak point: the sources for this instrument are mostly short mentions in lists; no detailed study of the kalikom was found.
